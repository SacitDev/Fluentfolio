6. WHAT THE DATA MEANS
- U012 is an utterance ID, A07 an answer, Q07 a question. Times (mm:ss) are positions in the ORIGINAL recording. Each ANSWER line also gives its start time in the audio file Fluentfolio made, when one was made.
- [pause 0.8 s] is a measured silence, placed before the next word. It is a measurement, not a judgement: decide from the words around it whether the candidate was planning content or searching for a word or structure. Pauses inside a clause matter more than pauses between ideas.
- uh, um: fillers as the recogniser wrote them. The HEADER says how reliable filler capture is. Do not count pause markers as fillers.
- wor- = cut-off word. [?word] = the recogniser was unsure; never a vocabulary or grammar error (for Pronunciation, see section 5). [overlap] = both people spoke; excluded. [question read aloud] = solo mode; not assessed. [non-English] = excluded. [withheld for audio check] = hidden on purpose.
- Rates are syllables per second, counted from the transcript. Faster is not better, and fewer pauses is not automatically better. No number maps to a band: never write rules such as "N words per minute = band X", and never put a rate and the word "band" in the same sentence, not even to say that the rate does not decide the band.
- MATTR = vocabulary variety in 50-word windows (0 to 1). Rare-word share = content words outside the 2,000 or 5,000 most common English words. Rare is not the same as good: check that each word is used correctly.
- Repetition, self-correction, structure and words-to-check lists are POINTERS: check each one in the transcript (or by ear) before you use it.
- Pitch range is the spread between the 20th and 80th percentile of the candidate's pitch, in semitones, combined from up to three tools with fixed weights. Its label says how far the tools agree: agree = usable; approximate or single_tool = use only for broad statements such as "fairly flat" or "lively"; unreliable or too_little_speech = no number is given, ignore it.
- Context measures (median pitch, pitch range, flat stretches, phrase endings, loudness) describe the voice and have no band meaning on their own. Use them with audio, or, without audio, only as clues for Pronunciation (section 5).
- kind and reliability labels: measured, estimated, pointer, context; high, medium, low. Lean less on evidence marked low.

8. WHAT TO LOOK FOR (our own checklist; the descriptors decide the band)
FC: keeps going and gives long turns (Part 2 length and how it ended: the HEADER "part 2 long turn" line); how much hesitation and why; repetition and self-correction (checked); range and overuse of linking words and discourse markers; relevance and logical development; whether fluency holds in Part 3.
Answering the questions (part of FC): answers what was asked; extends answers with reasons, examples and detail; covers the points on the Part 2 card; gives and supports opinions in Part 3.
LR: range on familiar (Part 1) and abstract (Part 3) topics; uncommon words and idioms, used correctly; collocation and style; paraphrase when stuck; wrong word choice (confident words only).
GRA: how often complex sentences appear beside simple ones (the structure examples are pointers); control of basic forms (verb tenses, agreement, articles, plurals); how many utterances have no errors; systematic versus occasional errors: a basic-form error that comes back often across answers shows the form is not yet controlled, so it weighs more than mistakes in complex sentences, and a few correct complex sentences do not make up for it, while a few slips that do not affect meaning fit higher bands too; any speech model can drop small errors such as endings and articles, even in a verbatim-best-effort transcript, so their absence is not proof of accuracy; self-corrections; possible memorised chunks (comment only).
P (by ear with audio; otherwise from the clues in section 5): how easy the candidate is to understand and how much effort the listener needs; individual sounds (words to check are hints for listening); word and sentence stress; intonation; chunking; rhythm and linking. An accent is not an error: judge only its effect on understanding.
